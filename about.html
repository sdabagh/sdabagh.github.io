About Me

Mom of five, statistics teacher, and the person behind Learn Without Walls

My Journey

Hi, I'm Safaa Dabagh. I'm a Lebanese American mom of five, and I teach statistics in Los Angeles.

I came to the United States with my three oldest children, wanting a better life for all of us. I started at West Los Angeles College, and the teachers there believed in me long before I believed in myself, and they kept pushing me to aim higher.

From there I transferred to UCLA and earned my bachelor's degree in Mathematics/Economics with a minor in Statistics. That is where I fell in love with statistics, because it lets you take real data about real people and actually see what is going on.

I stayed at UCLA for my master's degree in Statistics. Then I lost my mother, and I stepped away for a while to take care of my family and to grieve.

Where I Am Now

Today I teach introductory statistics at three campuses in Los Angeles, and I built Learn Without Walls so anyone can learn for free. My research looks at what helps community college students succeed in math, mainly placement reform and equity, and what changes when AI helps design a course. The students I have in mind are the ones sitting in front of me every week.

This work is personal for me. I know what it feels like to be underestimated, and to carry family and school at the same time while wondering if you even belong in the room. So I build things students can really use, and then I study whether they help.

My Family

My five children, Adam, Aya, Abed, Issa, and Zachariah, are my greatest inspiration. They watched me study and struggle and keep going, and I hope what they took from it is that it is never too late to go back to school.

Raising five kids while teaching full time and running this site is a lot, and some days it is too much! It has also taught me patience and what really matters.

My children are why I do this work. I want them, and every student, to grow up where a good education does not depend on money, and where AI helps teachers instead of replacing them.

Teaching Experience

I teach mostly statistics, at more than one college, and that keeps my research honest. I see what my students carry into class: money worries, jobs, families, gaps in their math background, and the quiet feeling that they do not belong.

I also see how hard they work. Many of them are working parents, new immigrants, or the first in their family to go to college. They deserve materials that start where they are.

Current and Recent Positions

  • Santa Monica College (2016 to Present): Math C1000 (Introduction to Statistics, formerly Math 54), including a section paired with the Math 54C support course; previously Math 4/4C (College Algebra for STEM)
  • West Los Angeles College (Current): Math C1000 (Introduction to Statistics) for the POPP program
  • Loyola Marymount University (Current): Math 205, Applied Statistics in R (two sections)

In my classes, mistakes are part of learning and no question is a silly question. Teaching at three very different colleges shows me how differently students come to statistics, and I learn from every one of them.

Teaching Philosophy

I teach statistics because I believe it is one of the most powerful tools a citizen can hold. Statistical literacy allows people to question claims, weigh evidence, and make decisions for themselves, rather than accepting what society tells them or what society says they are capable of. For that reason, I see mathematics education as a matter of equity: it belongs to every student, not to a few.

My own path shapes this belief. My college education in the United States began at West Los Angeles College, the same college where I now teach, and continued at UCLA, where I earned degrees in mathematics, economics, and statistics. I know from experience that a classroom can change the direction of a life.

My practice rests on three commitments. First, access: no student should be held back by cost, which is why this website offers free, advanced materials, including open textbooks and Stats Without Walls, a free statistical app I built. Second, representation: in 2020, I was part of the team that created Statistics for Social Justice, an open workbook from the ASCCC OER Initiative that shows students who does statistics. Third, students as creators of knowledge: in my Statistics for Journalism class at Santa Monica College, students helped shape a study of mass shootings across the states, and their idea to compare rates per person became the starting point of my research.

Above all, I see the classroom as the place where students discover themselves as leaders. They deserve a better education system, and that change begins with each of us as educators. When students ask the questions, build the analysis, and defend their conclusions, they are not only learning statistics; they are practicing the leadership our world needs from them.

My Values

Educational Equity

A good education should not depend on what you can pay. Technology should close gaps, not make them wider.

Lifelong Learning

I am still a student myself, in statistics, in teaching, and in parenting. Right now I am learning how to use AI well in my own classes.

Research for Good

Research should help people. I want mine to answer real questions about students and to feed into free materials they can use.

Compassion

Behind every data point is a person. I try to remember that in class, in my research, and at home.

What Drives Me

I was a community college student before I was a teacher, and I still remember what it felt like to sit in those seats. That memory is in everything I build here, and it is the reason the courses, the books, and Stats Without Walls are free.
